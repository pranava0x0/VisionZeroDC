## Part 2: Additional Policy & Infrastructure Ideas

### **Category A: Speed & Design (Beyond Road Diets)**

#### **1. Speed Limit Harmonization with Design**
- **Concept**: Match speed limit to geometry rather than arbitrary number
- **Mechanism**: In areas where design can't support 25 mph safely, reduce limit to 20 mph + physical enforcement (narrowed lanes, chicanes, center islands)
- **Evidence**: London Vision Zero paired 20 mph zones with street calming; compliance + culture change
- **Cost**: $500K–$1M per mile (engineering-integrated)
- **DC application**: Wards 7, 8 neighborhood streets; school routes
- **Equity**: Protects residents in historically underinvested areas

#### **2. Truck Routing & Curfew Program**
- **Concept**: Time-of-day restrictions on heavy vehicles; formalized loading zones
- **Mechanism**: Restrict trucks on Georgia Ave / South Capitol St during peak pedestrian/bike hours (7–10 AM, 3–6 PM)
- **Evidence**: Vancouver, Portland reduced conflicts + injuries with truck management
- **Cost**: Low (policy + enforcement); $200–500K for signage/loading zones
- **Bonus**: Improves air quality, reduces double-parking crashes
- **Political angle**: Business support (faster turnaround from fixed loading) + equity (safer for workers, pedestrians)

#### **3. Intersection Re-Signalization (Adaptive + Detection)**
- **Concept**: Real-time signal timing responsive to pedestrian, cyclist, and vehicle flows
- **Mechanism**: Detect presence of vulnerable users; extend crossing time; reduce "dilemma zone" rear-end crashes
- **Evidence**: DC already tested adaptive signals; Portland & SF report 10–15% injury reduction
- **Cost**: $150K–400K per intersection
- **Quick win**: Pair with existing signal-retiming projects (marginal cost)
- **DC corridor priority**: New York Ave NE (high pedestrian volume, multiple conflict points)

#### **4. Curb-to-Curb Width Reduction (Optical Narrowing)**
- **Concept**: Paint or physical narrowing to reduce *perceived* road width → lower speed
- **Mechanism**: Psychological effect; drivers naturally slow for narrower streets
- **Evidence**: Seattle, San Francisco report 2–5 mph speed reduction from optical narrowing alone
- **Cost**: $1–2K (paint) to $50K (semi-permanent barrier)
- **Quick deployment**: Wards 7, 8 main streets
- **Advantage**: Testable before capital projects; reversible

#### **5. Modal Priority Zones (Transit, Bike, Ped)**
- **Concept**: Streets where public transit, cycling, and walking are prioritized over private vehicle flow
- **Mechanism**: Bus lane enforcement, protected bike lanes, pedestrian plazas in curb space
- **Evidence**: Paris "15-minute city" model; lower speeds, higher safe-mode usage
- **Cost**: $500K–$2M per corridor (varies by scope)
- **DC fit**: Georgia Ave (K St to Petworth); South Capitol (coord with BRT if planned)

### **Category B: Enforcement & Compliance (Beyond Cameras)**

#### **6. Graduated Penalties for Repeat Speeding (DL Points + Insurance)**
- **Concept**: Stacking consequences for repeat violators (3+ camera citations in 6 months)
- **Mechanism**: 
  - 1st violation: Camera ticket
  - 2nd: Camera ticket + license point
  - 3rd+: License point + insurance surcharge + court option
- **Evidence**: Virginia, New York report compliance uplift; 15–20% reduction in repeat offenders
- **Cost**: Low (administrative); coordinate with DMV/insurance regulators
- **Equity safeguard**: Income-based fine reduction; public transit subsidy for suspended-license holders
- **Political**: Tough on dangerous drivers, not poor drivers

#### **7. Pedestrian Intersection Audits + Community Reporting**
- **Concept**: Formalize the process for residents to flag dangerous intersections
- **Mechanism**:
  - Mobile app or web form: report near-miss, broken signal, sight-line obstruction
  - DDOT triages; community group (ANC, Bread for the City) co-validates
  - Public dashboard shows reports + status (investigating, fixed, in queue)
- **Evidence**: NYC 311 system; Hamburg's "Mobility Check" app
- **Cost**: $50–150K (app dev + backend)
- **Equity**: Amplifies East-of-River voices; makes invisible problems visible
- **Bonus data**: Feeds into hotspot refinement

#### **8. High-Speed Corridor Enforcement Strike Teams**
- **Concept**: Intensive, visible enforcement on top 5 hotspots (not rotation, but concentrated)
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Deploy marked MPD vehicle + camera unit on New York Ave, South Capitol St for 4 weeks
  - High visibility (yellow vests, signs); goal is deterrence + speed reduction
  - Track speed before/after; public reporting
- **Evidence**: NYC "Summer of Safety" 2023; visible enforcement + media = behavior change
- **Cost**: $100–200K (MPD + equipment for 4-week blitz)
- **Equity frame**: "Wards 7, 8 deserve the same enforcement attention"
- **Politics**: Quick win, dramatic data, local ownership

### **Category C: Equity & Access**

#### **9. Free/Subsidized "Safer Route" Transit Passes**
- **Concept**: Residents in high-crash areas get reduced/free transit to avoid dangerous streets
- **Mechanism**: Partner with WMATA; issue subsidized SmarTrip cards in Wards 7, 8
- **Evidence**: Reduces car travel (fewer drivers = fewer crashes) + increases safe-mode access
- **Cost**: $500K–$1M annually (subsidy pool)
- **Equity**: Direct; targets highest-burden populations
- **Co-branding**: "Vision Zero Safe Routes" partnership between DDOT + WMATA

#### **10. Community Wealth Building via Safety Jobs**
- **Concept**: Hiring from high-crash neighborhoods for safety jobs (crossing guards, street auditors, installation labor)
- **Mechanism**: 
  - Recruit crossing guards + "safety monitors" (community members paid to observe/report safety issues)
  - Offer job training for protected intersection installation, bike lane painting
  - Partner with local nonprofits (Bread for the City, community colleges) for pipeline
- **Evidence**: Chicago INVEST program; New Orleans community repair corps
- **Cost**: Embedded in crossing-guard expansion ($1.8M/year) + training ($200K)
- **Equity**: High; direct employment in communities bearing the harm
- **Political**: "Good jobs for DC residents" narrative

#### **11. Transportation Justice Fund**
- **Concept**: Community investment pool for grassroots safety ideas
- **Mechanism**:
  - $500K annual budget; community groups propose small-scale interventions
  - Examples: ANC-led community street audits, pop-up parks in crash zones, youth bike safety education
  - Transparent grants process; local control
- **Evidence**: Santa Monica, San Francisco Justice funds
- **Cost**: $500K/year (competitive grants)
- **Equity**: Puts resources in community hands; builds leadership
- **Political**: Genuine community partnership

---

### **Category D: Prevention & Education**

#### **12. School-Based Driver Education Integration**
- **Concept**: Add Vision Zero curriculum to mandatory driver's ed for DC teenagers
- **Mechanism**:
  - Partner with DC Department of Education + driving schools
  - Require 2-hour unit on: speed-injury relationship, vulnerable user visibility, crash consequences
  - Include video of real DC crash sites; interview with victim/family
- **Evidence**: Australia + Canada programs; attitude shifts stick post-licensing
- **Cost**: Low (curriculum design + teacher training = $100K one-time)
- **Reach**: ~5,000 new drivers/year in DC
- **Equity**: Reaches all teens equally (not just wealthy ones in private schools)

#### **13. Workplace Safety Pledges (DC Employer Network)**
- **Concept**: Large DC employers (tech, government, nonprofits) sign Vision Zero pledge
- **Pledge includes**:
  - Safe routes to work promotion (transit subsidies, bike parking, walking routes)
  - Driver safety training for fleet (Uber, delivery, city vehicles)
  - On-site safety education (lunch-and-learns on pedestrian awareness)
- **Evidence**: SF Employer Network; 10–15% reduction in employee commute injuries
- **Cost**: Low (coordination); employers bear subsidy cost
- **Reach**: ~100K DC workers
- **Co-benefits**: Employer recruitment/retention, ESG goals

#### **14. "Vision Zero Champions" Resident Ambassador Program**
- **Concept**: Train 20–30 residents from high-crash neighborhoods as safety advocates
- **Mechanism**:
  - 6-week training (paid: $500/person) on: crash data, intervention mechanics, community organizing, policy
  - Graduates become official DDOT/Vision Zero consultants
  - Conduct community audits, speak at ANC meetings, design interventions with engineers
- **Evidence**: Vision Zero practitioner model (Seattle, San Francisco); community voices shape priorities
- **Cost**: $50K (training + stipends)
- **Equity**: High; puts leadership development in communities
- **Political**: "Residents leading their own safety solutions"

## Part 3: Policy Implementation Sequencing

### **Year 1 Priorities (Quick Wins + Foundation)**
1. **Speed limit + design harmonization** (Wards 7, 8 pilot)
2. **Truck routing curfew** (Georgia Ave, South Capitol St)
3. **Intersection re-signalization** (5–10 high-volume intersections)
4. **Enforcement strike teams** (4-week blitz on hotspots)
5. **Crossing guard expansion** (59% gap closure)
6. **Transportation Justice Fund** launch (community proposals)

### **Year 2 Expansion**
1. Complete protected intersections (4–6 sites from design phase)
2. Protected bike lane network (Georgia Ave, H St, 14th St)
3. Complete streets redesigns (2–3 corridors)
4. School-based driver education rollout
5. Employer safety network launch
6. Vision Zero Champions graduate cohort 1 actions

### **Year 3+ Sustained**
1. Maintenance + optimization (before/after analysis feeds loop)
2. Emerging interventions (autonomous vehicle guidelines, delivery robot zones)
3. National Vision Zero policy wins (federal funding, legislative model)
4. Equity + health outcomes tracking (disparities closing?)

## Part 5: Success Metrics & Accountability

### **Dashboarding**
- **Real-time**: Speed reduction (automated counters), enforcement transparency (camera tickets by location)
- **Quarterly**: KSI by corridor, mode breakdown, equity metrics (ticket distribution, crossing guard coverage by race/income)
- **Annual**: Before/after evaluation (paired treated/control corridors), community satisfaction surveys, injury costs avoided

### **Accountability Mechanism**
- **Vision Zero Oversight Board**: DDOT + Council + ANC + community orgs
- **Monthly public meetings** in rotating high-crash wards
- **Annual public report**: progress on 5-year goals, equity disparities, budget tracking

## Summary: 14 New Ideas + Tier-1 Implementation

**Quick wins (Year 1)**: Truck curfews, adaptive signals, strike-team enforcement, speed-limit design sync, crossing-guard expansion.

**Medium lift (Year 1–2)**: Complete streets, protected intersections, protected bike lanes, Workforce development (safety ambassador program, community jobs).

**Cultural shifts (Year 2–3)**: Driver education integration, employer partnerships, Vision Zero Champions program, Transportation Justice Fund.

**Equity as architecture, not afterthought**: Every intervention includes a disparities audit, community hiring, and transparent enforcement data.
