## The 5 Priority Next Steps (Ordered by Start Date)

### 🔴 **START NOW (Week 1–2)**

#### 1. **Verify Hotspot Coordinates** *(Week 1–4)*
- **What**: Confirm crash locations at intersection level; publish on map
- **Why**: Precision matters for design. Ward-grain too coarse for interventions.
- **Owner**: Data team
- **Cost**: $5–10K | **Timeline**: 4 weeks
- **Deliverable**: GeoJSON map layer of verified intersections
- **Success**: All 5 corridors mapped; 80%+ location confidence

#### 2. **Establish Evaluation Framework** *(Week 1–8)*
- **What**: Define metrics (crashes, speeds, equity), baseline collection, statistical methods — BEFORE building anything
- **Why**: Without this, we'll never know if our fixes work. Sets up rigorous before/after study.
- **Owner**: Evaluation team (university partner preferred)
- **Cost**: $30–50K | **Timeline**: 8 weeks
- **Deliverables**: Framework document, baseline data, pre-intervention photos/video
- **Success**: Framework approved by DDOT, MPD, external reviewer

#### 3. **Publish Enforcement Data Dashboard** *(Week 2–10)*
- **What**: Make speed-camera tickets + violations data public, disaggregated by location/time
- **Why**: Transparency + equity audit. Enables community accountability for camera placement disparities.
- **Owner**: DDOT data team + public affairs
- **Cost**: $40–70K | **Timeline**: 10 weeks
- **Deliverables**: Public interactive dashboard, monthly auto-refresh, CSV exports
- **Success**: 500+ unique visitors first month; community feedback form active
- **Equity angle**: Flag if any racial/income disparities in ticket distribution; recommend policy adjustment

---

### 🟠 **Phase 2 (Month 2–4)**

#### 4. **Launch School-Zone Crossing-Guard Expansion** *(Recruitment Week 1, Training Week 1–3, Deployment Week 4)*
- **What**: Hire crossing guards + pair with infrastructure (curb extensions, LPI) at 5–10 pilot schools in Wards 7, 8
- **Why**: Addresses 59% gap in coverage; highest ROI for child safety. Equity priority.
- **Owner**: DDOT + DC DoE + community partners
- **Cost**: $150–200K (pilot); $2–3M (full expansion)
- **Timeline**: 12 weeks to full pilot deployment
- **Deliverables**:
  - Job description + training curriculum
  - "Safe Routes to School" website
  - Baseline safety assessment for each pilot school
  - Community kickoff event at each site
- **Success**: 100% hiring by school year start; parent surveys show improved safety perception; 0 near-misses

---

### 🟡 **Phase 3 (Month 3–9)**

#### 5. **Design Protected Intersections** *(Design Month 1–6, Permitting/Community Month 3–9)*
- **What**: Detailed engineering design for 4–6 protected intersections on New York Ave NE + South Capitol St
  - Corner islands to block turning vehicles
  - Setback crossings to shorten pedestrian crossing
  - All-pedestrian signal phase (no simultaneous turns)
- **Why**: 50–70% reduction in turning-related fatalities at peer cities
- **Owner**: DDOT design team + civil engineer consultant
- **Cost**: $600K–1.8M per intersection; Phase 1 design: $600K–1.8M (4–6 intersections)
- **Timeline**: 6–9 months (design only; construction Year 2)
- **Deliverables**:
  - 30% design plans for each intersection
  - Community engagement summary + design modifications
  - ADA accessibility certification
  - Cost + construction timeline + funding strategy
  - Grant applications (federal FHWA, local foundations)
- **Success**: 4–6 designs approved + funded; construction start within 12 months

## The "Quick Wins" Ideas (6–12 Months)

**Bundle these into annual repaving to extend impact citywide:**

### Low-cost, High-impact Treatments
1. **Daylighting** (clear sight corners): Remove parking within 20 ft of crosswalk → $1–2K per intersection
2. **Curb Extensions** (bump-outs): Shorten pedestrian crossing distance → $5–15K each
3. **Leading Pedestrian Intervals** (LPI): 3–7 sec head start for pedestrians → Signal timing only ($500 hardware)
4. **High-Visibility Crosswalks**: Thermal plastic or paint → $1–2K per crossing
5. **School-Adjacent Parking**: Timed loading zones near schools → $2–5K per school

**How to scale**:
- Identify annual repaving schedule (DDOT does ~5–10 miles/year)
- Add $50–100K/mile for quick-win bundle
- Prioritize Wards 7, 8, East Capitol St corridor
- **Reach potential**: 10–20 miles citywide within 5 years

## The "Aspirational" Ideas (Year 2+)

### **Protected Bike Lane Network**
- **Corridors**: Georgia Ave NW (3 mi), 14th St N/S (2 mi), H St NE (2 mi)
- **Why**: 44% injury reduction vs. painted lanes; 75% ridership bump
- **Cost**: $400K–600K per mile = $2–3M for network
- **Timeline**: 12–18 months design + build
- **Equity**: Schools + transit hubs connect car-free; enables working parents

### **Community Safety Leadership Program**
- **What**: Train 20–30 residents from high-crash neighborhoods to design & advocate for their own street safety
- **Why**: Solutions designed WITH communities, not FOR them, stick longer
- **Activities**: Walk audits, participatory design, Council testimony, ongoing monitoring
- **Cost**: $50–100K (trainer + stipends)
- **Timeline**: 2 mo recruitment + 2 mo training + ongoing

### **Rapid Transit Integration (BRT)**
- **What**: Coordinate protected intersections + hardened pedestrian islands with planned BRT on Georgia Ave or South Capitol St
- **Why**: BRT + Vision Zero signals "this corridor is being redesigned for safety, not just cars"
- **Cost**: Overlaps with BRT capital (~$100M+); Vision Zero adds $500K–1M in safety treatments
- **Timeline**: Coordinate with BRT design (months 4–8)
